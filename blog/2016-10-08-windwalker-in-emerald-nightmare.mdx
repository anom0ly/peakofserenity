Last few weeks have been quite crazy; the legion launch was an undeniable success. We've been overwhelmed with content, world quests, things to test and optimize. Work, mythic + and mythic raiding have taken quite a huge share of my time lately and I haven't been able to release things at the pace I'd wanted. As a result of this, encounter guides are still being done, lots of things remain unanswered.

Now that I'm done with mythic Emerald Nightmare myself, I wanted to catch up a bit on that. However I also wanted to do a more general blogpost covering more personal opinions on Windwalker, it's current spot in raids/dungeons and maybe some answers to things you might be wondering a bit.\
This is that blog post.

## Legion launch, Windwalker tuning

Windwalker rushed very brutally into legion, carried by it's new toy, Strike of the Windlord. We were given one of the hardest hitting spells in the whole game, on a short cooldwon (reduced by serenity), at low cost. On top of that, devs decided to make it pure AoE with a huge hitbox.\
This made windwalkers infamously overpowered for the first week of legion, finishing dungeons with 40-60% of groups damage regularly and ending several packs on ten or twenty millions dps.\
This kind of made spinning crane kick and Fists of Fury feel useless in comparison, as [we had kinda predicted](http://www.walkingthewind.com/2016/06/06/panda-in-legion-4-aoe-tools/): there was no room for that many crazy AoE spells.

The nerf followed shortly and was brutal, pretty much removing the AoE part of Strike of the Windlord and making it very strong at single target and cleave up to 5 targets. This change was probably necessary, wasn't large enough to prevent the fact that we help a lot with dungeon clear speed and made the rotation feel way smoother.\
The good thing is that Windwalker didn't suffer from any further nerfs. Our single target damage is very highly competitive, specially considering how quickly we benefit from a second or third target (which are legion). Windwalker wasn't given crazy utility, but we kept our niche of doing large AoE damage without being turned into single target noodles. I am grateful for this because this makes a huge difference in between us being on the bench and seeing play.

## Serenity and Storm, Earth, and Fire The next big question is level 100 talents. I expect most readers to be adressed to this article because of this. Before legion launch, several windwalkers amongst whom Megabloks notified me they solely used Serenity in Legion dungeon testing because of how buggy they felt SEF was. They found it insanely strong. [I have myself](http://www.walkingthewind.com/2016/08/17/serenity-theorycrafting/) been a huge fan of this new iteration of the spell and claimed it would be crazy strong on 2-5 targets due to the large amount of ressources it brought there, whereas SEF brought nothing. Besides, in dungeons, if you're slamming serenity on cooldown (which you'll often be in higher mythic+ dungeons), then the two stacks mechanic of SEF brings way less than it does in a raid encounter. 1 extra cast over 5 minutes is way more relevant than 1-2 more over 30 minutes. However, at that time, we also warned people that with very little efforts put into the APL, simcraft was showing serenity to be barely 2-4% behind whirling dragon punch. This meant that if SEF suffered from a couple bugs (and trust me, it did in beta), it would likely fall behind. Of course the two stacks mechanics of SEF is very strong and was supposed to prevail in a progress environement. Turns out, there were quite a lot of "bugs" with Storm, Earth, and Fire on release. Despite us being told during alpha testing that since SEF replaced our dps cooldown (tiger eye brew), it would be fixed quickly if anything didn't benefit from it/didn't work with it, it was not the case. Here is a list of some of these issues with the spell: -SEF clones do not benefit from haste: This is probably the most annoying one to me. Clones have a set haste level and do not channel any faster as you gain some. This includes the benefits from bloodlust. Therefore, if you use Fists of Fury then immediately use rising sun kick at the end with bloodlust up, you're likely to have just canceled 2 out of 5 ticks of Fists of Fury from both your clones. That's a 27% dps loss on Fists of Fury, meaning without spiritual focus it's a 1% dps loss to use FoF with SEF rather than without if you're not aware of this. That's pretty sad for a "dps cooldown". -SEF clones stack their own hit combo: I heard at some point this might be considered as intentional. I find this hard to believe that so many little things would be intended to reduce the mechanics around which the class is balanced to the point where you doubt whether using your DPS cooldown is a good idea or not. -SEF clones attacked banished targets, even in a cluster of crowd controled targets: This is more of a QoL one, but just like I disliked when 33% of my damage attacked the immuned Eye of Il'Gynoth, I dislike it even more when my earth spirit goes to Fists of Fury a banished target and breaks a couple paralysis/hexes in the process.
