So we are approaching the one year anniversary of the transition from a variety of sites into one unified Monk site. I'm sure I'm not alone in saying that the past year has absolutely blown me away with what we've been able to accomplish. In the 11 months since the site went live to the public on roughly March 27th, 2017, here are the stats:

- Over 4.15 million views and nearly 650,000 visitors. **That's an average of 1900 visitors and 12,278 views per day!!**
- 126 new articles, roughly 2.6 articles per week, **FOR 48 WEEKS!!**
- 151 pages.
- 12 Authors with currently live articles.
- 1900 comments from users and authors on current articles.
- Donations have generated almost $6,000 for our authors. Enough that I had to claim it on my taxes this year since everything technically goes to me and the US Gov doesn't care if I send it to you fine people. My overall % share of the monthly amount continues to go down as content is more spread out among more authors, which is exactly what I was hoping for.

These numbers are pretty crazy from top to bottom. PeakofSerenity has become as close to a "gold standard" for class-based sites as any other site. I've had hundreds of compliments about how great Peak is, even some saying we're the best site across several games people play.

However, the work is not yet done. There is much more we can do to improve the site, and (largely) make our lives easier going into Battle for Azeroth. As you well know, I put out a survey to the community to get their feedback on the site; what we did well, what we did not so well, and what we can improve upon. The survey is at around 700 responses, so definitely enough to draw statistically significant conclusions from.

- And the spreadsheet with some complied information: [https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1UMHzr6pKf_zwcA3b0FBmmcB--erfooWpk1Jgy1IEOJA/edit?usp=sharing](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1UMHzr6pKf_zwcA3b0FBmmcB--erfooWpk1Jgy1IEOJA/edit?usp=sharing)

#### Survey Results

I'll leave the spec specific things to each spec's manager and authors to look at what can be improved upon. Much of it shouldn't be a surprise if you've been paying any attention to people's comments. So here's some information that I'm taking away from the survey:

##### Demographics

- 70% of the users play Windwalker, 52% play Brewmaster, and 47% play Mistweaver. That's mostly in line with current demographics from around the web. There's always things that can change in BfA, but over time, the population of viewers from each spec, and the corresponding views per spec page/post has continued to balance more and more as the largest population at the start was Windwalker by a landslide. Things have continued to normalize, which I'm very happy with.
- The overall rating people gave the site is 8.5; which is just about exactly where I'd like us to start and improve from. Obviously an 8.5 out of 10 is a fantastic rating, but I know we can get that closer to 9, and should be constantly looking to improve.
- Possibly the most surprising part of the whole survey; users have been playing Monk for a conservative average of 14.3 months. Only 8.2% have been playing less than 3 months and 71.5% have been playing longer than a year. This will obviously fluctuate as (hopefully) more people come to Monk with the new expansion. This also means that we don't have to focus as much on the mundane basics as we can assume some prior knowledge and experience when we provide our content. We won't skip the basics, but can assume the majority doesn't need them.
- Users have been using Peak, WtW, or CraneStyleHealing for 8.4 months, with 17% using Peak for less than 3 months, and only 44% using it for more than a year. This means that the majority of users came to Peak and didn't experience what came before. Whats important about this is that Peak is all they've known, so we can't rest on "Well its better than WtW" as they may not have experienced it. Peak is their baseline.
- Only 22.7% reported that they came from WtW/CSH, 32.8% came from Discord, 18.9% from a web search, 10.7% from word of mouth, and the rest from a smattering of other sources. This led me into looking at where Peak falls in search engine results, and how we can improve the chances that people end up with us through that avenue, which I'll address further down.
- Finally, I looked at the type of content that our users regularly partake in for each spec. These were largely uniform, with the exception of more people switching to Windwalker for WQ relative to the representation of the other specs. Unsurprisingly, middle difficulty Mythic+ dungeons, Heroic and Normal Raids, and WQ were the activities that more than 50% of our users partake in on a regular basis. Mythic raids were 43%, with "Cutting Edge" Mythic raiders only making up 20.52% of the respondents, albeit typically the most vocal portion, but far from the majority. Also unsurprisingly, only 20-30% of users take place in any form of PvP, which may be a symptom of overall demographics or the fact that there are other dedicated site specific to PvP.

##### Site Usability

- People rated us an average of 4.16 of how easy the site is to navigate to find what they need. As with the overall rating, this is a good place to start, although I would have preferred slightly higher to start with.
- 60.8% of respondents said they prefer that guides be organized in one page, with sections or tabs devoted to each guide topic, rather than separate pages. We split them into multiple pages to make it easier to link to the specific topic. However, they may be ways to keep everything localized in one location and use anchors, tabs, or other functions to still have the ease of linking the information people are looking for. Another possibility may simply by to make the multiple pages easier to navigate between. I'd certainly like other people's thoughts on that.
- 42.2% said they learn their information best through text, with 46.8% preferring a mix. This, of course, isn't surprising, and something we can explore in the next expansion with providing other resources beyond those in text.
- This was possibly the most important part of the survey for me, but 42.9% of respondents said they prefer for information to be presented in TLDR format as well as full explanation, but have them separate. 46.4% said they prefer both, but put them together. At Juko's behest, I split those preferences up based on the content people say they do. Raiders and Mythic+-ers prefer TLDR and Full explanations to be separate whereas Dungeoners and PvPers prefer them to be together. These numbers are basically close enough that we can pick one direction and stick with it, without much of an issue. Obviously the overwhelming majority of 89.3% of respondents prefer that we provide both forms of information. This is largely consistent with user's ratings of each type of content we provide, with things like Mechanics and Ability spotlights being rated higher than even our PvE Guides (4.05 rating vs 3.98).

#### Site Improvements

So with all this wonderful data there are some improvements I want to make to how the site operates so that we can better provide content.

##### Search Engine Optimization

One of the things that I looked into through all this was how Peak is ranked compared to other sites when someone does a Google search. Here's how we rank with what I imagine are common search terms.

- Spec - "Brewmaster": 8th, "Mistweaver": 5th, "Windwalker": 5th
- Spec + "Monk" - "Brewmaster Monk": 5th, "Mistweaver Monk": 11th (second page), "Windwalker Monk": 6th
- Spec + "Monk Guide" - "Brewmaster Monk Guide": 5th, "Mistweaver Monk Guide": 9th, "Windwalker Monk Guide": 5th

Peak ranks pretty high most of the time, and I excluded some videos that were posted in the search. There also could be some variation since I'm sure Google spies on all of us and skews my search results to what it thinks we're looking for. Generally the main problem I had after looking at the search results is that we're always below Noxxic. Being behind Icy-Veins or WoWhead is understandable as they are much larger productions with more resources to put into optimization. But Noxxic is just not a quality website, so if there are improvements we can make to get closer, if not pass Noxxic, I think those will be changes worth making.

1. More "obvious" titles and descriptions - This is one of the more "obvious" things we can do to make sure that our content, mainly our guides, are more easily searchable. If its a "Guide" for "Mistweaver Monks" then we can make sure that is included in the URL, title, or first part of the page. This will make it more likely that anyone searching for "Mistweaver Monk Guide" or any combination of those words comes to our pages.
2. Use this plugin Yeost - If you've looked to write a post in the last few days, you may have noticed that I have enabled the website plugin Yeost. This is designed to help us find ways we can improve our posts and pages. Some of these suggestions are easy to implement, others are more annoying, but they \*should\* all help people to find our resources. And the more people that find our stuff on their own, the less people we have to answer questions with a link to it ourselves.
3. Bug Fludders - I \*mostly\* jest, but there are many computer and web people among us, and others of us with almost no knowledge of how Google reads my mind and knows what I'm searching for. If the goal is to make our resources more readily find-able, and thus cut down on how much work we all have to do hand feeding it to people, I'm sure that's a goal everyone can get behind.

These are some very basic improvements that we can make to help people find our stuff, and more importantly help curb the amount of people coming to us quoting Noxxic.

##### Transitioning to BfA

There are some more changes that I'd like to make to the site as we transition from Legion to BfA. With 6 months or so before the next expansion, including most of the northern hemisphere's summer, this gives us a bunch of time to get things ready so that when BfA does come out, the site is in a good shape and we're not scrambling to put things in place then. Here are some things I'd like to happen before BfA

1. Figure out who is doing what - The original idea was to have people somewhat do whatever they want and have one person oversee the content for each spec. With how much traffic the site is getting, I think that continues to be fine for posts, but guides need to be maintained and updated regularly. This means that I'm going to be more on top of the guide pages for each spec and making sure that they're updated and maintained.
2. Update front page - Its pretty now, it can get better. I know Cele was trying some things out with it, and its starting to look real nice.
3. Convert any Spotlight posts into pages - Some mechanics that we've already covered in spotlight articles will be carrying over into BfA. These articles can either be just copy/pasted into a page, or they can be cleaned up a smidge and put into guides or a unified "in-depth" explanation page. For example, Windwalker's Hit Combo and Mastery is currently going to carry into BfA, I'll simply be taking my [Hit Combo spotlight article](/2016/07/26/spotlight-hit-combo-and-mastery/), cleaning it up a bit and making it a permanent page, rather than a post.
4. Define universal guide format - This will be one of the biggest things that will help the site. Right now all three specs mostly use their own method of organizing the guides. This can make it somewhat difficult for players who use more than one spec to find information that is in one place for a spec, and another place for the others. Before BfA I/we'll come up with a unified guide template, similar to how websites like WoWHead do it, this will be much easier with how many of the things that required their own section being left behind in Legion. This will include a "changelog" section so that \*hopefully\* people stop asking if/when the guide was last updated.
5. Purge all old stuff - This is something that wont' happen till we're fully set up and transitioned to the BfA version of Peak, but its definitely something I'll be looking to do, so anything that should be saved will need to be marked as such or migrated over to pages to be saved.

##### Questions for Discussion

So there are a few things that I want to be discussed over the next few weeks and months, things that I can't quite come up with an answer for.

1. Guide Organization - In the years I've been running a site and writing guides, I've tried several methods of structuring the guides and none seemed to be the silver bullet or holy grail that I'm looking for. This is definitely something that we can brainstorm, pull examples from other sites, or just make them up.
2. Theme/template changes - The Divi theme seems to work pretty well, even if it requires some minor tweaking. Is this something we want to stick with? Are there things we can improve upon in the theme of the site?
3. Site Income - Although it isn't the main focus of the site, and I doubt anyone is in this for the money, I'm surely not the only one who enjoys getting a few extra dollars, euros, krone, or whatever your countries use. Its a form of validation for the days and hours we all spend working on the site and in the community. However, the site has steadily been losing support through Patreon since it peaked in August, after some small up/down from May to August. This doesn't seem to be connected to any decrease in content the site providing nor a content drought in the game. Even though site views jumped nearly 50% with the opening of Antorus, donations to the site have slowly fallen around 5% a month since August. I don't know if this is a problem worth spending time on, or one that we can control, but it may be something worth discussing.
4. Income Split - To piggyback off #2, the current model of dividing the income the site produces is based on page views. This seems to work well enough to \*theoretically\* divide the income by the amount of content that an author provides, but it may not be the biggest incentive or reward for someone who does less frequent or more niche content. Does this continue to be the best way to fairly divide any money that the site generates? Is there a better option?

I'm sure that there is some other things I'm missing that will come to me over the next few months. I am incredibly happy and proud of all that we've been able to put together, and its made even better by the fact that there's a dozen other people working on making this site the best it can be. We have an opportunity with the new expansion to really raise the bar for all community sites and, more importantly, ourselves, while simultaneously setting things up to be more effective and efficient in the future. If you've made it this far then understand how grateful I am for everything you do for the site and for the community. Hopefully this has helped to energize you and get people excited for all that we can do in the coming months and new expansion.
